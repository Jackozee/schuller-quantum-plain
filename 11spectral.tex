Recall that the resolvent map \(R_A\colon\rho(A)\to\Cal L(H)\) sends
an element \(z\in\rho(A)\) to the bounded operator
\begin{equation}
  R_A(z) = (A-z)^{'} \colon \Cal H\to\Cal H
\end{equation}
where \((A-z)'\) is the composition
\begin{equation}
  (A-z)' = i_{\Cal D_A}\circ(A-z)^{-1}
,\end{equation}
of the inverse map \((A-z)^{-1}\colon\Cal H\to\Cal D_A\) and the
inclusion \(i_{\Cal D_A}\colon\Cal D_A\to\Cal H\).

Assume that \(A\) is self-adjoint. In this case we have
\(\rho(A)\subseteq\R\), so the resolvent map \(R_A\) is defined on
\(\H=\{z\in\C : \Im z > 0\}\).
Now, for every \(\psi\in\Cal H\), define the function
\(N_\psi\colon\H\to\C\) by
\begin{equation}
  N_\psi(z) = \langle \psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle.
\end{equation}
One can prove that \(N_\psi\) is analytic on \(\H\). Moreover, it
satisfies
\begin{align}
  \Im N_\psi(z)
  &= \frac{
    \langle\psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle
    -
    \langle R_A(z)\psi,\psi\rangle
  }{2i} \\
  &= \frac{
    \langle\psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle
    -
    \langle\psi,R_A(\bar z)\psi\rangle
  }{2i} \\
  &= \frac{ \langle\psi,(R_A(z)-R_A(\bar z))\psi\rangle }{2i} \\
  &= \frac{ \langle\psi,(z-\bar z)R_A(z)R_A(\bar z)\psi\rangle }{2i} \\
  &= \langle\psi,\Im(z)R_A(z)R_A(\bar z)\psi\rangle \\
  &= \Im(z)\langle R_A(z)\psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle \\
  &= \Im(z)\|R_A(z)\psi\|^{2}
,\end{align}
where we have used that \(R_A(z)^{*}=R_A(\bar z)\) and
the \emph{first resolvent formula}
\begin{equation}
  R_A(z)-R_A(w)=(z-w)R_A(z)R_A(w)
.\end{equation}
Since \(R_A(z)\) is injective, it follows that \(\Im N_\psi(z)>0\)
whenever \(\Im(z)>0\) (we say \(N_\psi\) is a Herglotz-Nevanlinna
function).
A classical theorem
(see \cite[\S69, Thm 2]{akhiezer1961operators})
tells us that any function with this property can
be written in the form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nevanlinna}
  N_\psi(z)
  = \alpha + \beta z +
  \int_{\R}^{} 
  \left(
    \frac{1}{t - z}
    -
    \frac{t}{1 + t^{2}}
  \right)
    \d\mu_\psi(t)
\end{equation}
where the measure \(\mu_\psi\) is given
by the Stieltjes inversion formula
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:inversion}
  \mu_\psi(-\infty,t]
  =
  \lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}
  \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}
  \frac{1}{\pi}
  \int_{-\infty}^{t+\delta}\Im N_\psi(\tau+i\epsilon)\d \tau
.\end{equation}

In fact, we have the following:
\begin{proposition}
  The representation \eqref{eq:nevanlinna} of
  \(N_\psi(z)=\langle\psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle\) takes the simpler form
  \begin{equation}
    N_\psi(z)
    =
    \int_{\R}^{} \frac{\d\mu_\psi(t)}{t - z}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Note that \(N_\psi\) satisfies the additional condition
  \begin{equation}
    \sup_{y>0} |yN_\psi(iy)|<\infty.
  \end{equation}
  Since this supremum is finite, we have an upper bound \(M\)
  for the absolute values of the real and imaginary parts of
  \(yN_\psi(iy)\):
  \begin{equation}
    \left\{
      \begin{aligned}
        \left| \alpha y + y \int_{\R}^{}
        \left(
          \frac{t}{t^{2}+y^{2}}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}
        \right) \d\mu_\psi(t)
        \right| &\leq M,
        \\
        \left| \beta y^{2}
          + y^{2}\int_{\R}^{} \frac{1}{t^{2}+y^{2}} \d\mu_\psi(t)
        \right| &\leq M,
      \end{aligned}
    \right.
  \end{equation}
  Dividing the first inequality by \(|y|\), the second by \(y^{2}\), and
  taking \(y\to\infty\), we obtain
  \begin{equation}
    \alpha = \int_{\R}^{} \frac{t\d\mu_\psi(t)}{1+t^{2}},
    \qquad
    \beta = 0,
  \end{equation}
  which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Note that the Stieltjes inversion formula allows us to construct the
measures \(\mu_\psi\) using only the function
\(N_\psi(z)=\langle\psi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle\), which in turn is
calculated in terms of \(A\).
By polarization, we can also construct the complex measures
\(\mu_{\phi,\psi}\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \langle \phi,R_A(z)\psi\rangle
  =
  \int_{\R}^{}\frac{\d\mu_{\phi,\psi}}{t-z}
\end{equation}
and the unique measure \(P\colon\Sigma_\R\to\Cal L(\Cal H)\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \langle \phi, P(\Omega)\psi\rangle = \mu_{\phi,\psi}(\Omega).
\end{equation}
One must show that this \(P\) is a projection-valued measure,
and that the integral \(\int_{M}^{}\lambda\d P(\lambda)\) coincides
with \(A\) again (both the domain and the action on vectors).
